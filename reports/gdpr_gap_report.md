# GDPR Compliance Gap Report

## Summary

- **Partial**: 4
- **Gap**: 12

## Details

### Article 5 — Principles relating to processing of personal data
**Verdict:** Partial

While Policy Sections 2 and 3 specify what data is collected and how it is used for specified purposes, the policy completely lacks any mention of data accuracy, storage limitation (retention periods), or the overarching principles of lawfulness, fairness, and transparency as required by Article 5.

### Article 6 — Lawfulness of processing
**Verdict:** Gap

While Policy Section 3 outlines the purposes for using data (such as processing orders and sending marketing), the policy completely fails to state the specific legal bases relied upon for each type of processing as required by Article 6.

### Article 7 — Conditions for consent
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections completely fail to address the conditions for consent, the requirement to demonstrate that consent was given, or the mechanism to withdraw consent as outlined in Article 7. Policy Section 6 only mentions general inquiries via email, which does not constitute an adequate process for managing or withdrawing consent.

### Article 9 — Processing of special categories of personal data
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections do not mention special categories of personal data, explicit consent, or the narrow legal exceptions required by Article 9. Sections 2 and 3 list standard collection and usage of general data, while Sections 6 and 7 do not address sensitive data processing.

### Article 12 — Transparent information and communication
**Verdict:** Partial

While Policy Sections 2, 3, and 4 outline data collection, usage, and sharing in a structured manner, the provided text does not explicitly address the requirement to use concise, easily accessible, and clear language specifically tailored for transparency as mandated by Article 12. Furthermore, it lacks explicit communication channels or procedures for how data subjects can easily exercise their information rights.

### Article 13 — Information to be provided (data collected from the subject)
**Verdict:** Gap

While Policy Sections 2, 3, and 4 cover the controller's data collection, purposes, and recipients, the provided sections entirely fail to mention the identity of the controller, the legal basis for processing, the retention period, or the data subject's rights as required by Article 13.

### Article 15 — Right of access
**Verdict:** Gap

While Policy Section 6 invites users to contact support with questions about their data, it fails to outline the specific right of access, including how to obtain confirmation of processing, access to the data itself, and details regarding processing purposes, categories, and recipients as required by Article 15.

### Article 16 — Right to rectification
**Verdict:** Gap

The policy mentions contacting support with questions in Policy Section 6, but it completely fails to explain how individuals can request the correction of inaccurate personal data as required by Article 16.

### Article 17 — Right to erasure ('right to be forgotten')
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections do not mention the right to erasure or how deletion requests are handled. Policy Section 6 only mentions contacting support with general questions, which is insufficient to address the specific requirements of Article 17.

### Article 20 — Right to data portability
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections do not mention the right to data portability, how individuals can request to receive their data in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, or how they can transmit it to another controller. Sections 2, 3, 4, and 7 cover data collection, usage, sharing, and security, but fail to address the requirements of Article 20 entirely.

### Article 24 — Responsibility of the controller
**Verdict:** Partial

Policy Section 7 mentions taking reasonable measures to protect personal information, but this is extremely vague and fails to detail specific technical and organizational measures to ensure and demonstrate comprehensive compliance as required by Article 24. The remaining sections outline data use, sharing, and collection, but do not address the overarching governance and demonstration of compliance.

### Article 25 — Data protection by design and by default
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections mention generic data protection in Policy Section 7, but they do not address building safeguards into systems from the outset or ensuring default settings are the most privacy-protective option as required by Article 25.

### Article 30 — Records of processing activities
**Verdict:** Gap

While Policy Sections 2, 3, and 4 touch upon categories of data, purposes, and sharing, the policy completely fails to mention maintaining an internal written record of processing activities or address retention periods as required by Article 30.

### Article 32 — Security of processing
**Verdict:** Partial

Policy Section 7 mentions taking reasonable measures to protect personal information from unauthorized access, but it remains vague and fails to detail specific technical and organizational security measures like encryption or risk considerations as required by Article 32.

### Article 33 — Notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections do not address the requirement to notify a supervisory authority of a personal data breach within 72 hours. Sections 2, 3, 6, and 7 cover data collection, usage, rights, and security measures, but contain no information regarding breach notification procedures.

### Article 37 — Designation of the data protection officer (DPO)
**Verdict:** Gap

The provided policy sections completely fail to mention the designation or contact information of a Data Protection Officer (DPO), which is required by Article 37. Sections 2, 3, 4, and 7 address data collection, use, sharing, and security, but contain no reference to a DPO.
